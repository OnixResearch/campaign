# Risk analysis for build-campaign-core

## Assets and hazards

### History and policy identity

Campaign decisions depend on exact Choregraph history and one explicit Campaign control lineage.

Hazards include stale branch generations, ambiguous lineage, crossed graph identities, conflicting control events, and incomplete identity framing.

### Frontier integrity

The frontier determines which candidate can execute next.

Hazards include duplicate candidates, duplicate selection ordinals, stale guidance, lost outstanding work, hidden removal, invalid children, and non-deterministic tie breaks.

### Guidance and budgets

Consumers provide priority facts and resource costs.

Hazards include malformed vectors, overflow, negative-cost misuse, stale guidance, unbound entropy, false resource observations, and product semantics entering shared policy.

### Publication fencing

A decision must become durable before action execution.

Hazards include action execution from an unpublished plan, stale compare-and-set updates, concurrent planner races, and selection receipts treated as execution evidence.

### Ownership overlap

Choregraph owns immutable history, branch references, replay, merge, and optional Universal Weave topology.

Hazards include a second history engine, duplicate replay authority, Campaign-owned topology, or an ambient worktree dependency.

### Consumer authority

Consumers own operation meaning, execution, retries, results, findings, snapshots, and evidence.

Hazards include generic action DTOs that hide product constraints, Campaign retry policy, false finding claims, and shared receipts promoted into product evidence.

### Non-VM candidate authority

The Animus-shaped fixture supplies opaque candidates and validator references. BranchContext supplies exploration patterns only.

Hazards include treating ranks or worker exits as acceptance, importing Python or FUSE effects, and equating a validator reference with a passing validator.

The fixture must prove deterministic selection without execution or promotion authority. Animus and Lattice retain their current validation and acceptance gates.

### Dynamic bounds

Exploration can discover work until a resource bound ends selection.

Hazards include unbounded frontier growth, event retention, depth, guidance records, finding records, and control history.

## Mitigations

- The Choregraph adapter consumes one published immutable history revision and exact schema.
- `campaign-core` does not depend on Choregraph or Universal Weave types.
- Campaign reconstructs state from one explicit control lineage and bound exploration projection.
- Domain-separated BLAKE3 framing binds history, policy, adapter, state, candidate, budget, and decision identities.
- Checked arithmetic covers every selected resource dimension.
- Canonical ordering and candidate identity provide deterministic tie breaks.
- Exploratory selection receives explicit bounded entropy and never reads ambient randomness.
- Selection publication uses an exact immutable control event and generation-fenced branch-reference plan.
- Consumers cannot execute from a planned but unpublished selection.
- Reusable candidates, retirement, unique selection instances, and bounded outstanding work remain separate.
- Pruning affects only available derived candidates. It cannot rewrite or delete history.
- Findings remain opaque and non-terminal unless an explicit stop policy applies.
- Infrastructure retry retains the original selection identity and remains consumer-owned.
- Replay and minimization remain outside Campaign.
- Positive tests cover roots, dynamic candidate admission, selection, observation, forks, budgets, findings, pruning, cache use, and stop decisions.
- Negative tests cover stale history, ambiguity, duplicate candidates, crossed observations, overflow, early execution, history rewrite, and overclaims.
- Every accepted state keeps control-event headroom for each outstanding settlement and one stop. The adapter keeps the same reserve against the host's projection bound, the branch generation range, and the Choregraph graph's event limit. It admits a control root only with room for that root's stop, and it returns the same admission when an identical root is retried while the graph holds no other Campaign control event under the admission's envelope. It republishes an already admitted identical draft idempotently.
- The adapter accounts for one campaign per graph and envelope. It refuses a control root on a graph that already holds another Campaign control event under the same envelope, and it refuses to validate a graph that holds a second control root under the envelope, both with `SharedControlGraph`.
- The adapter reserves every uncommitted control draft that descends from the branch head: a pending draft, drafts chained below it, and racing siblings. A stale publisher or a compare-and-set loser is refused with `PendingControlDraft` before it admits a draft, so it cannot consume the reserve of the lineage the branch adopted. Planning refuses with the same error until the host completes the pending drafts through `pending_control_draft`. Hosts publish a control event only on a durably committed branch value (`campaign.publication_fence.durable_base`), so the drafts they leave are a partial commit and racing siblings, which recovery completes within the recorded reserve. A validated projection counts each descending draft as a selection and its settlement in the reserve it records, and it exposes the room other writers may still use (`graph_room`). Once that reserve no longer fits, a publication that no longer fits is refused with the capacity error, and so is every plan that core planning accepts once no draft is pending; a pending draft still answers `PendingControlDraft` first.
- Publication, advance, and cache validation go through the held projection once one exists. That path rejects rewinds, renames, fork jumps, forged fences, envelopes under foreign identities, and graphs older than the held projection's (`StaleGraph`).
- A campaign ranks one guidance rank shape under a total order, so production and the reference planner agree. Rank orders that name a dimension beyond that shape are refused before any entropy draw is read.
- Receipts certify only re-planned decisions or canonical recorded events under the admitted branch and current generation.
- The infra checker refuses every Cargo configuration file and toolchain `path` in the repository outside its own fixture roots (`tools/campaign-infra-checker/fixtures`), compile-time reads and source indirection in every token tree of the shared crates, feature changes on allowlisted core dependencies in any manifest, and compiled targets outside their member. It exempts a module from the history, consumer, and replay scans only when every declaration naming it is gated by exactly `#[cfg(test)]` or the file's own inner `#![cfg(test)]` removes it outside tests, and it exempts items gated by `#[cfg(test)]` the same way; it counts the pinned consumer constants only while a registered, armed test runs the suite. It is a lexical guard over ordinary repository changes.
- Conformance requires the consumer shell to give Campaign's own answer over generated input classes, including the whole history catalog through every held-matrix holder and every stop reason on the claims oracle. Each review round's reproducers of repaired findings remain as named regression tests and negative controls, except where `verification.md` records otherwise (the round-6 host-capacity receipt reproducer was not kept, because that finding is accepted as a documented limit).

## Dependency acceptance

The public Choregraph source supplies the history contract at `b3e08e19750f53bdbcae970cdf58a47a791ed20b`.

Campaign pins that full revision. The source record is `provenance/CHOREGRAPH-PUBLICATION.md`, which records a public fetch of that revision on 2026-09-06.

During closeout the Choregraph forge (`git.onix.computer`) answered HTTP 530. The closeout gates therefore ran from the pinned source and the other git inputs in the local Cargo git cache and Nix store, and they do not re-prove that the pinned revision is publicly available.

Source availability does not prove adapter behavior. Campaign must pass exact identity, projection, publication-fence, and consumer conformance checks before stable release.

## Non-claims

This list is the canonical non-claim list of the change; the non-claim lists in `design.md` and `verification.md` match it.

- **Release readiness.** No gate, audit round, or record here shows that Campaign is ready for stable publication, production use, or release. Campaign's final `nix flake check -L` passed at `fc05da20`, after the accepted-spec sync (see `evidence/final-gate.md`). Stable publication still needs the archived lifecycle changes. The ChaosControl consumer evidence is recorded (`ntmtnrtq`) against R5, a development pin (see **Residual risks**).
- **Exhaustive search.** A frontier or budget stop, a clean audit round, and a passing conformance suite do not show that any search space, or the space of adversarial inputs, is exhausted.
- **Test sufficiency.** Passing tests show only the behaviour they exercise. They do not show that the tests, the conformance generators, or the audit rounds are sufficient.
- **Fresh acquisition.** The closeout gates ran from cached sources while the Choregraph forge answered HTTP 530. They make no claim that the pinned Choregraph revision or the other git inputs can be fetched from their public sources today; public availability is as recorded in `provenance/CHOREGRAPH-PUBLICATION.md` on 2026-09-06. ChaosControl's adoption gates likewise ran with Campaign R5 preloaded into its caches; fresh acquisition of R5 from the live endpoint was not observed.
- **Event truth and payload authenticity.** Choregraph identity and Campaign receipts establish structural binding to exact history, not that a recorded event, observation, or payload is true or authentic.
- **Operation success and external execution.** A selection decision claims no operation started, completed, or produced a result (`campaign.selection.non_claim`), and no receipt claims operation success or deterministic external execution.
- **Replay success.** No receipt or decision claims that a replay ran or succeeded; replay stays outside Campaign.
- **Finding correctness.** Findings are opaque consumer references; Campaign claims nothing about their meaning or correctness.
- **Authorization.** No receipt or decision claims authorization. A consumer executes a selection only after its durable publication and keeps its own execution authority.
- **Safe deletion.** Pruning removes only available derived candidates and retirement blocks new selections, and neither deletes history; no receipt claims that anything may be deleted.
- **Consumer correctness.** Conformance compares a consumer shell with Campaign's answers over the inputs the suite presents; it does not show that the consumer is correct.
- **Stop attribution.** Stop events and a recorded stop carry no selection profile, so their receipts, including those of a `PolicyRequested` stop, bind whichever valid production profile the request names and do not attest that this profile's stopping rule produced the stop (`campaign.receipts.crossed`).

Every Campaign receipt lists the eight receipt non-claims of `campaign.receipts.non_claims` (the `NonClaim` enumeration): exhaustive search, event truth, operation success, replay success, finding correctness, authorization, release readiness, and safe deletion.
